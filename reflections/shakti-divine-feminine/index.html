Shakti: Awakening the Divine Feminine Within Us

Navratri, Shakti, Shiva, Kundalini and the invitation to become whole
By Rev. Bill McDonald · Approx. 10 min read
In brief

Rev. Bill McDonald describes Shakti as the living energy within every person, not only a goddess to worship. He explores Durga, Kali, Lakshmi and Saraswati, Shiva and Shakti, and Kundalini, and asks whether awakening makes the heart more loving, compassionate and whole.

The Invitation of Navratri

There are times in the human journey when the world seems to be asking us to remember something we have forgotten. Navratri is one of those times.

In the Indian tradition, Navratri is a sacred period honoring the Divine Mother in her many expressions. It is a celebration of Shakti—the feminine power, energy and creative force of the universe. For some, this is a religious festival. For others, it is an ancient cultural tradition. But Shakti can also be understood in a much deeper and more universal way.

Shakti Is Within Us

Shakti is life itself. It is the energy that moves through creation, the power behind birth, growth, transformation and renewal. It is the force that brings something invisible into visible form. We see it in the mother giving birth, the artist creating, the healer touching another human being, the mystic going inward, and the heart opening after years of being closed.

Shakti is not simply a goddess somewhere outside of us. Shakti is within us.

The Many Faces of the Divine Feminine

The ancient Indian traditions gave the Divine Feminine many names and faces because no single image could contain its mystery. Durga represents the strength and protective power of the Divine Mother. Kali represents transformation, the fierce destruction of ignorance and the courage to let go of what has died within us. Lakshmi represents abundance, beauty, generosity and the grace that allows life to flourish. Saraswati represents wisdom, knowledge, music, creativity and the flow of consciousness into expression.

These are not merely stories about supernatural beings. They can also be understood as different expressions of the energy that lives within every one of us. There is a Durga within us that finds courage when life becomes difficult. There is a Kali within us that must sometimes have the courage to destroy an old pattern, an illusion or an identity that no longer serves our awakening. There is a Lakshmi within us that knows how to appreciate beauty, gratitude, generosity and the abundance of life. And there is a Saraswati within us that seeks knowledge, wisdom, creativity and truth.

The Divine Mother has many faces because consciousness itself has many expressions.

When Power Loses the Heart

This is where the meaning of the Divine Feminine becomes important in this changing age. We have lived for a long time in a world that has placed enormous value on masculine principles of achievement, competition, control, conquest, knowledge and power. These qualities are not wrong. We need courage, discipline, reason, structure, strength and the ability to act. But something becomes unbalanced when these qualities exist without the heart.

Knowledge without compassion can become cold. Wisdom without love can become another form of ego. Power without conscience can become destructive. Intelligence without an understanding of the human heart can create systems that are efficient but not necessarily humane.

The Divine Feminine reminds us of something different. It reminds us to feel, to listen, to nurture, to create, to receive, to have compassion and to trust intuition. It reminds us that life is not merely something to be conquered, measured and controlled, but something to be experienced and honored.

Qualities That Belong to Every Human Being

And I believe these qualities belong to every human being. They are not exclusively women’s qualities.

A woman does not have to become less feminine to develop strength, courage, reason and wisdom. A man does not have to become less masculine to open his heart, become compassionate, nurturing, intuitive or emotionally present. The deeper spiritual truth is that we contain more than the limited identity we have created around our physical bodies. We are Spirit having a human experience.

Within that experience are masculine and feminine principles, and both are necessary. The body may be male or female. Our personalities may express different qualities, and our cultural roles may be different. But at the level of consciousness, the Divine does not divide itself into separate compartments.

There is no male God and female God. There is simply the One.

Shiva and Shakti

The ancient Indian teachings understood this beautifully through Shiva and Shakti. Shiva represents pure consciousness, stillness and awareness. Shakti represents the dynamic energy that moves through that consciousness. Shiva is the silent witness; Shakti is the movement of life. One without the other is incomplete. Stillness without movement cannot create, while movement without stillness has no direction.

This same principle exists within us. We need both.

Kundalini and the Real Question of Awakening

I have always been fascinated by the relationship between consciousness and energy because I have experienced enough of life to know that there is much more happening around us and within us than what the physical senses can easily explain. We are not merely bodies walking around having thoughts. There is an energy moving through us, and the more conscious we become, the more sensitive we can become to that movement.

This is where Kundalini enters the picture.

Kundalini is often spoken about as a mysterious spiritual force lying dormant at the base of the spine, waiting to awaken. There are many interpretations of Kundalini, and I believe it is important not to reduce it to a particular physical sensation or supernatural experience. To me, Kundalini is part of the deeper awakening of consciousness—the awakening of the energy within us that has been asleep beneath our ordinary identification with the body, personality and ego.

Is the Heart Opening?

When consciousness begins to awaken, the real question is not whether we have unusual sensations or mystical experiences. The real question is what happens to the person. Do we become more loving? More compassionate? More truthful? More forgiving? More aware of the Divine within ourselves and others?

If spiritual energy is awakening but the heart remains closed, something important has been missed.
The purpose of awakening is transformation.

As consciousness awakens, we begin to see differently and question the old stories we have told ourselves. We become less interested in proving who we are and more interested in discovering who we really are. The heart begins to open. Intuition becomes clearer. Compassion becomes less theoretical and more natural. We begin to sense that the Divine is not somewhere far away waiting for us to become worthy enough to find it. The Divine is already here. It is within us.

The awakening of the Divine Feminine is therefore not about turning men into women or women into something different. It is about becoming more whole.

I have spent much of my life exploring energy, consciousness, meditation, healing and the mysterious relationship between the physical and spiritual worlds. One thing I have come to understand is that energy is not limited by the categories we create with the human mind. Love has no gender. Compassion has no gender. Intuition has no gender. Creativity has no gender. The desire to nurture and protect life has no gender. The ability to surrender to something greater than ourselves has no gender. These are human expressions of something much greater.

Fierce and Tender

We also have to understand that feminine energy does not mean simply being soft. There is tremendous power in Shakti.

Durga is not weak. Kali is not weak. The mother who gives birth is not weak. The person who protects those they love is not weak. The person who has the courage to love after being hurt is not weak. There is enormous power in the heart.

The Divine Feminine can be gentle, but she can also be fierce. She can nurture life, but she can also destroy what no longer serves life. She can give birth, but she can also transform.

This is why compassion alone is not enough. Compassion without action can become passive. Love without wisdom can create problems. Forgiveness without discernment can become an invitation for continued abuse. Nurturing without boundaries can become self-abandonment.

On the other side, wisdom without love can become judgment. Strength without compassion can become domination. Knowledge without the heart can become arrogance.

We need the balance.

This balance is part of spiritual maturity. The awakened human being does not reject one side in favor of the other. He or she learns to bring the two together—the marriage of consciousness and energy, wisdom and love, strength and compassion, masculine and feminine.

This is not about abandoning the natural strengths of being male or female. It is about becoming complete human beings without imprisoning ourselves inside rigid definitions of what a man or woman is supposed to be.

A man can be strong and tender. A woman can be nurturing and fierce. A man can possess great wisdom and still listen to his intuition. A woman can be deeply compassionate and still possess extraordinary power and discernment. These qualities do not cancel one another. They complete one another.

Bringing the Heart Back Into the World

Perhaps this is one of the great lessons our world needs right now.

We have incredible knowledge. We have more information available to us than any generation in human history. We can communicate across the planet in seconds. We can see distant galaxies, create extraordinary technologies and collect data about almost everything.

But information is not wisdom, and wisdom without love is incomplete.

Our governments need this understanding. Our corporations need it. Our schools need it. Our religious institutions need it. Our families need it. And perhaps most of all, we need it within ourselves.

Imagine a world where decisions were made not only by asking, “What is possible?” or “What is profitable?” or “What gives us more power?” but also by asking: What is compassionate? What preserves human dignity? What protects life? What brings people together rather than separating them? What does the heart know that the numbers cannot tell us?

This does not mean abandoning reason. It means bringing the heart back into the conversation.

The world does not need less intelligence. It needs intelligence guided by compassion. It does not need less strength. It needs strength guided by love. It does not need less science. It needs science accompanied by wisdom and reverence for life.

It does not need men to abandon their masculine nature or women to abandon their feminine nature. It needs both men and women to rediscover the fullness of who they are.

Perhaps this is what the ancient image of Shiva and Shakti is trying to teach us. Consciousness and energy are not enemies. They are partners in creation. The silent awareness and the creative force belong together. Every human being carries a sacred meeting place within themselves where these two principles can come into balance.

The masculine and feminine are not two separate worlds fighting for control. They are two expressions of one Divine reality.

The Divine Feminine is not a political idea. It is not a competition between men and women. It is a spiritual principle. It is the remembering that life is sacred, that creation is sacred, that the heart is a source of knowledge, that intuition has a place alongside reason, that nurturing is a form of strength, that compassion is not weakness and that love is not sentimental.

Love is energy. Love is power. Love is the force that allows consciousness to recognize itself in another.

Awakening the Mother Within

Perhaps this is why the ancient traditions continue to speak to us after thousands of years. They were not merely teaching us to worship an image of the Divine Mother. They were pointing toward something within ourselves.

Navratri can therefore become more than a celebration on the calendar. It can become an invitation to awaken the Shakti within us—to awaken the Durga that gives us courage, the Kali that helps us release what must die, the Lakshmi that teaches gratitude, generosity and appreciation for the abundance of life, and the Saraswati that inspires us to seek wisdom, truth and creative expression.

And beneath all of them is the deeper consciousness that knows we are never truly separate from the Divine.

The journey of awakening is not about becoming something we are not. It is about remembering what we have always been.

Behind the personality is consciousness. Behind the ego is the heart. Behind the human form is the Divine. And within that Divine consciousness there is no separation.

There is no male consciousness and female consciousness. There is only Oneness expressing itself through countless forms.

Perhaps the great awakening of this new age will not come simply through acquiring more knowledge. Perhaps it will come when knowledge and love finally meet, when wisdom learns to bow to compassion, when strength learns to serve, and when power remembers responsibility.

When men and women discover that the qualities they have been taught to separate into masculine and feminine are actually parts of one human and spiritual whole, we may begin to understand what the ancient teachings were pointing toward all along.

This is Shakti.

This is the Divine Feminine.

And this is the invitation of Navratri—not merely to honor the Mother outside ourselves, but to awaken the Mother within.

Because the world does not need more separation. It needs more wholeness. It needs more heart. It needs more compassion. It needs the courage of Durga, the transforming power of Kali, the generosity of Lakshmi, and the wisdom and creativity of Saraswati. It needs the stillness of Shiva and the living energy of Shakti.

And perhaps, more than anything else, it needs us to remember that beneath all of our differences, we are still expressions of the same Divine energy—the One Life, the One Consciousness, the One Love moving through us all.

A Question to Carry With You

What quality of Shakti is asking to awaken more fully within you right now: courage, transformation, gratitude, creativity, compassion, discernment, or the ability to receive?
