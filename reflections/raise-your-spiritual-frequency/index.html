10 Things That Raise Your Spiritual Frequency

What “raising your frequency” means when we understand it as a quality of consciousness
By Rev. Bill McDonald · Approx. 8 min read
In brief

Rev. Bill McDonald uses “frequency” as a spiritual way of describing the quality of consciousness we live from, not something an instrument can measure. He offers ten practices, from stillness and acceptance to surrender, that move us from my will toward Divine Will.

What Rev. Bill Means by “Frequency”

There is a frequency within each of us that exists beneath the noise of the personality, beneath our fears, desires, opinions, identities and constant mental activity. When I use the word frequency, I am not referring to something that can be measured with a scientific instrument. I am using it as a spiritual way of describing the quality of consciousness from which we are living.

We can feel the difference between states of consciousness. Fear, resentment, jealousy, greed, constant striving and the need to control create one kind of inner state. Love, compassion, gratitude, presence, humility, acceptance and surrender create another.

A person can be outwardly successful and inwardly restless. Another may have very little and yet carry an extraordinary sense of peace. The difference is not necessarily what is happening around them. It is the state of consciousness they are inhabiting within themselves.

In this sense, raising our frequency means moving from the denser states of consciousness—fear, attachment, ego, resistance and separation—toward greater clarity, presence, love, compassion and connection with the Divine.

It is not about becoming “higher” than another person. It is not a spiritual ranking system. It is about becoming less divided within ourselves and more aligned with what is deepest and most sacred within us.

The ancient Eastern traditions understood that the spiritual journey is not really about becoming something we are not. It is about removing what prevents us from experiencing what is already within us. The yogis spoke of realization of the Self. Buddhism speaks of awakening. Taoism points toward harmony with the natural flow of existence. The mystics speak of union with God.

Different traditions, different language, perhaps pointing toward the same mystery.

Here is my list of 10 actions and behaviors that will raise our frequency and the quality of our consciousness:

01Enter Silence and Stillness

We live in a world of constant stimulation. Phones, television, social media, news, conversations and our own endless thinking keep the mind moving almost continuously. Yet beneath all of this activity is a quieter dimension of ourselves.

Meditation is one doorway into that silence.

When we become still, we begin to notice that we are not every thought that passes through the mind. We can become the witness rather than the thought, the awareness rather than the mental noise.

The ocean may have waves on its surface, but beneath the waves there is stillness. Our consciousness can be like that.

God does not need to speak louder. We need to become quiet enough to listen.

02Accept What Is and Flow With Life

Much of our suffering comes from resistance. We want people to behave differently. We want circumstances to change. We want the past to have been different. We want life to follow our plans.

But life is not obligated to follow our plans.

Acceptance does not mean passivity, weakness or approval of everything that happens. It means seeing reality clearly enough to stop fighting what has already arrived.

The Tao teaches us about flowing with the natural movement of life. The Bhagavad Gita teaches us to act according to our dharma without becoming attached to the results.

Sometimes the closed door is direction. Sometimes the unexpected event becomes the teacher. Sometimes the path we resisted becomes the path that transforms us.

We do not always need to understand the river before we learn how to flow.

03Purify What You Put Into Your Consciousness

Everything we take into ourselves leaves an impression.

Food affects the body. But so do the things we watch, listen to, read, discuss and repeatedly think about.

What are we feeding the mind?

Anger? Fear? Violence? Gossip? Constant political conflict? Envy? Endless distraction? Or are we feeding ourselves beauty, wisdom, nature, meaningful conversation, meditation, prayer, art, love and truth?

The yogic traditions speak of sattva—clarity, harmony, purity and lightness of consciousness. The principle is simple: what we repeatedly consume becomes part of the atmosphere of our inner life.

We can begin asking ourselves:

Does this make my consciousness clearer or cloudier?

Does it bring me closer to love or farther away from it?

Purification is not about becoming perfect. It is about becoming conscious of what we are allowing into the sacred space of our own mind.

04Live Your Dharma

Instead of constantly asking, “What do I want?” we can begin asking, “What is mine to do?”

In the Eastern traditions, dharma points toward our responsibility, our right action, our deeper purpose and the way we are called to live.

Dharma is not necessarily some grand spiritual mission. It may be raising a family, caring for someone we love, doing honest work, speaking the truth, keeping a promise, forgiving someone, serving another person or simply becoming a more conscious human being.

Our values become our spiritual practice.

Truthfulness. Compassion. Courage. Loyalty. Humility. Forgiveness. Service.

We raise our consciousness not by talking about these qualities, but by living them.

05Gather Your Scattered Energy

Our attention is one of our greatest forms of energy, yet we constantly give it away.

We think about yesterday while worrying about tomorrow. We are with one person while thinking about another. We are eating while looking at a screen. We are working while checking our phones.

Our physical body may be in one place while our consciousness is everywhere else.

Spiritual practice brings us back.

When you are with someone, be with them. When you eat, eat. When you walk, walk. When you meditate, meditate.

Presence gathers the scattered pieces of ourselves.

The more present we become, the less energy is lost in unnecessary thought, worry, fantasy and distraction.

06Release the Need to Become Somebody

The ego is always trying to become something.

More successful. More important. More respected. More admired. More beautiful. More powerful. More wealthy.

And even spirituality can become another costume for the ego.

We can begin believing that our experiences make us special. We can collect spiritual teachings, wear spiritual identities and convince ourselves that we have arrived somewhere others have not.

But the deeper spiritual path moves in the opposite direction.

We become less concerned with being somebody and more willing simply to be.

Less self-importance. Less defensiveness. Less need for recognition. Less attachment to identity.

The ego wants to become larger. Consciousness becomes clearer when the ego becomes quieter.

The goal is not to destroy the personality. It is to stop mistaking the personality for the whole of who we are.

07Surrender the Fruits of Your Actions

The Bhagavad Gita offers one of the great teachings of spiritual life: act fully but release your attachment to the fruits of your actions.

We still work. We still love. We still create. We still make plans and give our best.

But we stop making the outcome the condition of our peace.

Love someone without demanding that they love you in exactly the way you want.

Serve without requiring recognition.

Speak the truth without demanding that everyone agree with you.

Do what is right because it is right, not simply because it guarantees a reward.

This is the spirit of karma yoga—action without attachment.

We are responsible for the action.

We are not always responsible for the result.

08Choose Peace Over Being Right

The ego loves to win.

It wants the last word. It wants to prove its point. It wants to correct everyone. It wants to demonstrate that it knows more, understands more or has the better answer.

But sometimes being right costs us our peace.

This does not mean abandoning truth or allowing injustice. There are moments when we must speak, stand our ground and protect what matters.

But before entering another argument, we can ask:

Am I speaking because truth needs to be spoken, or because my ego needs to win?

Sometimes wisdom speaks.

Sometimes compassion speaks.

Sometimes silence speaks.

Sometimes walking away is not weakness but mastery of our own energy.

Not every battle deserves our consciousness.

09Practice Surrender

Surrender is often misunderstood.

It does not mean giving up. It does not mean becoming passive. It does not mean allowing others to control us or refusing to take responsibility for our lives.

There are deeper levels of surrender.

First, we surrender control. We recognize that we cannot control other people, circumstances, timing or outcomes. We can act wisely, but we cannot control the entire river of life.

Then we surrender resistance. We stop fighting against what has already happened. Acceptance does not mean approval. It means we stop wasting our energy arguing with reality.

Then we surrender attachment. We may still have dreams, preferences and desires, but we stop making our peace dependent upon getting exactly what we want.

And eventually we begin to surrender personal will.

Instead of continually asking, “What do I want?” we begin asking, “What is life asking of me?”

This is where surrender becomes spiritual practice.

Prayer becomes less about telling God what we want and more about becoming open to what God may reveal.

Meditation becomes less about achieving some extraordinary experience and more about becoming available to the truth already within us.

Surrender is not giving up. It is giving over.

10Move From My Will to Divine Will

Perhaps the deepest movement in spiritual life is the movement from “my will” to Divine Will.

But this does not happen all at once. The previous practices prepare us for it.

We surrender control.

We surrender resistance.

We surrender attachment.

We surrender the need to become somebody.

And eventually we begin surrendering the belief that our personal will must always be in charge.

The question changes.

What do I want?

Then:

What is mine to do?

And finally:

What would the Divine have me do?

This is not about destroying our individuality or becoming passive. We still choose. We still act. We still speak, love, work, create, protect and serve.

But we begin to recognize that the small self does not see the whole picture.

We see one moment.

The Divine sees the whole journey.

We see the immediate circumstance.

The Divine sees what may unfold beyond it.

In the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna guides Arjuna toward action without attachment and toward surrender to the Divine. The teaching is not to stop acting, but to act from a deeper consciousness.

Divine Will may not arrive as a booming voice from heaven. It may come quietly through conscience, intuition, compassion, synchronicity, circumstance, meditation or a deep inner knowing that does not need to shout.

But we cannot hear that guidance while constantly shouting our own demands.

This is why silence, stillness, acceptance, purification, dharma, presence, detachment and surrender are not separate practices. They prepare the consciousness to become receptive.

We become less concerned with making God agree with us and more willing to listen for what the Divine may be asking of us.

We become less controlling and more trusting.

Less demanding and more receptive.

Less concerned with being seen and more willing to serve.

Less interested in having our way and more interested in discovering the way.

The spiritual journey is not about destroying the self. It is about allowing the small self to become transparent enough that something greater can shine through it.

Perhaps this is what raising our spiritual frequency ultimately means.

Not climbing somewhere above ourselves.

Not becoming spiritually superior.

Not pretending to be enlightened.

But removing what blocks the natural light of consciousness.

Becoming still.

Becoming humble.

Becoming loving.

Becoming present.

Becoming willing.

And slowly, almost imperceptibly, my will begins to open into Divine Will.

The higher frequency was never somewhere above us.

It was always within us.

A Question to Carry With You

Which of these practices is life asking you to deepen right now?
