Relationship Advice for Modern Times

What stays sacred even as everything else changes
By Rev. Bill McDonald · 5 min read

We are living in changing times. The world is moving quickly, old customs are being questioned, relationships are being redefined, and people are discovering new ways of living and relating to one another. Change itself is not the enemy. Life changes. Society changes. We change.

But there are some things that should never become old-fashioned.

Loyalty should never go out of style. Respect should never become unnecessary. Honesty, kindness, compassion, and keeping one’s word are not outdated ideas from another generation. They are the foundation stones of trust between human beings.

Whether we are speaking of marriage, friendship, family, or any close relationship, people still need to know that they matter. They need to feel emotionally safe. They need to know that someone is standing beside them and not constantly looking for the nearest exit when life becomes difficult.

A relationship is not two people standing on opposite sides of a battlefield, trying to win every argument. At its best, it is two people learning how to become a team.

This does not mean we will always agree. We should not expect another person to think exactly as we think, believe exactly as we believe, or see the world through our eyes. Each soul has traveled a different road. Each person has been shaped by different experiences, joys, wounds, and lessons.

Respect means allowing another person the dignity of being different.

You can disagree without becoming enemies. You can have opposing opinions without humiliating one another. You can say, “I see this differently,” without making the other person wrong, stupid, or unworthy of your love.

This is one of the great spiritual challenges of our time: Can we remain loving when we do not agree?

Can we remain loving when we do not agree?

Real relationships require more than physical attraction, shared interests, or convenience. Human beings have emotional needs and spiritual needs just as surely as they have physical needs. We need affection, understanding, encouragement, acceptance, and the feeling that someone truly sees us.

The body may be fed and sheltered while the heart is starving. A person can lie beside someone every night and still feel profoundly alone.

This is why kindness matters so much. Kindness is not weakness. Compassion is not sentimental foolishness. They are forms of spiritual intelligence. A kind word spoken at the right moment can heal something that an argument will only deepen. Compassion allows us to remember that the person standing before us is often carrying burdens we cannot see.

Sometimes the greatest gift we can give another person is simply to listen. Not to immediately correct them. Not to prepare our defense. Not to prove that we are right. Just to listen with an open heart.

There are also ethical and moral responsibilities in every meaningful relationship. Freedom does not mean that we have no responsibility for how our actions affect others. Being modern does not mean that integrity is optional.

We still need loyalty. We still need honesty. We still need people who mean what they say and say what they mean. We still need friends who do not disappear when life becomes inconvenient. We need partners who understand that commitment has meaning. We need people who do not casually betray trust and then call it personal freedom.

The spiritual life is not separate from the way we treat one another.

You cannot meditate your way around a cruel heart. You cannot read sacred books while continually wounding the people who love you. You cannot speak beautifully about enlightenment and ignore the basic responsibility of being decent, compassionate, and trustworthy.

How we treat one another is part of our spiritual practice.

Perhaps this is the simplest teaching of all: love must eventually become visible. It must show itself in patience. In forgiveness. In loyalty. In kindness. In the willingness to work through misunderstandings rather than immediately throwing people away.

Of course, there are relationships that become unhealthy, abusive, or destructive, and no one should remain where they are being seriously harmed. But too often today, people abandon relationships simply because relationships require effort. We sometimes confuse temporary discomfort with a sign that something must end.

Every relationship will eventually ask something of us. It will ask us to grow. It will ask us to become less selfish. It will ask us to listen more carefully. It will ask us to forgive. It will ask us to remember that love is not merely something we feel when everything is going our way.

The deepest friendships and relationships are not built upon perfection. They are built when imperfect people decide that what they have together is worth caring for.

In this changing world, let us welcome what is new without carelessly throwing away what is timeless. Let us be modern without losing our manners. Let us be independent without becoming indifferent. Let us honor freedom while remembering responsibility.

And let us remember that beneath all the changing ideas about relationships, the human heart has not changed as much as we sometimes imagine.

We still want to be loved. We still want to be respected. We still want to be heard. We still want to know that we can trust someone.

These are not old-fashioned needs. They are human needs. And perhaps they are spiritual needs as well.

For when we truly begin to see the Divine in one another, loyalty becomes more than obligation. Respect becomes more than good manners. Kindness becomes more than politeness.

They become ways of honoring the Sacred Presence that lives within us all.
