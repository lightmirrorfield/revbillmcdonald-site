Loyalty

Remaining Faithful to What Is Worthy
By Rev. Bill McDonald · 11 min read
01 HUMILITY|02 LOYALTY|03 COURAGE|04 POSITIVE LIVING|05 TRUTHFULNESS|06 SELF-CONTROL|07 PURITY
← Previous Virtue 2 of 7 Next →
About This Reflection

Rev. Bill draws a clear line between true loyalty and blind allegiance. Loyalty, he writes, is not telling someone what they want to hear — it asks what we are faithful to, and whether that faithfulness can survive being told the truth.

If Humility teaches us to move beyond the smallness of the ego, Loyalty teaches us how to remain faithful to what is greater than the ego. Humility quiets the need to make everything about ourselves. Loyalty asks what we are willing to stand for, remain true to, and protect with our lives. It is one of the highest forms of love because true loyalty requires something from us. It asks for faithfulness when it is inconvenient, commitment when things become difficult, and integrity when no one is watching.

In a world where people can change their minds, their relationships, their beliefs and their commitments with a swipe of a finger, loyalty has become increasingly important. We live in a time when many people are encouraged to keep their options open, move on when something becomes difficult, and place personal comfort above commitment. Sometimes leaving is necessary and healthy. But there is also a great spiritual danger in constantly abandoning people, relationships, principles and commitments simply because something has become difficult.

Loyalty is not about being trapped. It is about knowing the difference between freedom and irresponsibility.

A spiritual life requires commitments.

We make promises to one another. We take vows. We make pledges. We give our word. We enter into marriages, friendships, families, organizations, communities and causes. We say, "I will be there." We say, "I will love you." We say, "I will stand beside you." We say, "I give you my word."

Our word should mean something.

Marriage is one of the clearest places where loyalty is tested. It is easy to love someone when life is exciting, when we are young, when the relationship is new and when everything seems to be going our way. The deeper question is what happens when illness comes, when money becomes difficult, when personalities clash, when attraction changes, when one person is hurting, or when life simply becomes ordinary.

Marriage is not only about romantic feelings. It is about two people making a sacred commitment to walk together through the changing seasons of life. That commitment does not mean tolerating abuse or remaining in a destructive situation. But it does mean understanding that love cannot survive if every difficulty becomes a reason to leave.

There are times when relationships must end. Loyalty does not demand that we remain where there is cruelty, betrayal or harm. But even when a relationship ends, we can leave with honesty, dignity and respect rather than hatred and revenge. We can keep faith with the truth of what the relationship meant without pretending that everything must continue forever.

Friendship carries its own form of loyalty. A true friend is not simply someone who entertains us or agrees with us. A friend is someone who can tell us the truth when we do not want to hear it. A friend can celebrate our success without jealousy and stand beside us when we fail without humiliation.

Loyalty does not mean telling someone what they want to hear.

Sometimes loyalty requires us to tell the truth.

There is a false loyalty that protects a person from the consequences of their actions. We see it in families, friendships, businesses, politics, religious organizations and even spiritual communities. Someone does something wrong, and those around them remain silent because they do not want to create conflict. They call it loyalty.

But silence in the presence of wrongdoing is not always loyalty.

Sometimes the most loving thing we can do is speak the truth.

Truth and loyalty belong together.

If I truly love you, I should not have to lie to you to prove my loyalty. If I am loyal to a friend, I should want that friend to become a better human being, not simply feel comfortable. If I am loyal to my family, I should not protect destructive behavior simply because we share the same blood. If I am loyal to an organization, I should be willing to tell its leaders when I believe something has gone wrong.

Loyalty without truth becomes blind allegiance. Truth without love can become cruelty.

The spiritual path asks us to hold both.

This is where loyalty connects with Humility. The ego wants loyalty when it benefits itself. It wants people to be loyal to our opinions, our version of events, our authority and our personal interests. Humility reminds us that we may not always be right. Loyalty then becomes something deeper than having people agree with us. It becomes faithfulness to what is true, even when the truth asks something of us personally.

There is also loyalty to family.

Our families are among the first places where we learn commitment. Parents sacrifice for children. Children eventually care for aging parents. Brothers and sisters support one another through the years. Families do not always agree, and they certainly do not always get along, but there can still be a thread of love that remains.

Family loyalty does not mean that we must approve of everything a family member does. It means that we do not casually abandon the people who have been entrusted to our lives. We remember where we came from. We remember those who helped us when we could not help ourselves.

And yet, even family loyalty must ultimately be guided by higher principles.

The same is true of loyalty to our country.

There is nothing wrong with loving one's country. There is something beautiful about gratitude for the land, people, freedoms, traditions and opportunities that have shaped our lives. Those who serve their country, especially those who place their lives in danger for others, understand something about commitment that cannot be learned merely from words.

But spiritual loyalty to country cannot mean abandoning conscience.

A country is made up of people. Governments are made up of people. Organizations are made up of people. Leaders are made up of people. None of them are Divine simply because they hold authority.

Our highest loyalty must remain with Truth, Love, Compassion, Justice and the Divine principles that transcend every institution.

When loyalty to an organization requires us to violate our conscience, we have crossed a line. When loyalty to a leader requires us to lie, we have crossed a line. When loyalty to a group requires us to hate another group, we have crossed a line.

The Divine is not limited to our side.

This is where the realization of Oneness becomes essential.

If there is One Divine Presence within all, then our loyalty cannot stop at the boundaries of our family, our religion, our political beliefs, our country or our social group. We may have particular responsibilities to those closest to us, but we cannot forget the larger human family.

The person on the other side of the argument is also part of the One.

The stranger is part of the One.

The person who believes differently is part of the One.

The person we do not understand is part of the One.

Loyalty to the Divine therefore expands our loyalty rather than shrinking it.

It teaches us to keep our commitments while remembering that every human being has Divine worth.

This is also why loyalty requires discernment. Not every promise should be kept simply because it was made. Not every vow is sacred simply because it was spoken. We must examine what we are pledging ourselves to.

A person can be loyal to an unhealthy relationship. A person can be loyal to a corrupt organization. A person can be loyal to a destructive belief. A person can remain loyal to an old identity long after that identity has ceased to serve their spiritual growth.

The question is not simply, “Am I loyal?” The deeper question is, “What am I loyal to?”

That question can change everything.

Are we loyal to our anger?

Are we loyal to our wounded pride?

Are we loyal to being right?

Are we loyal to old resentments?

Are we loyal to the story that we are a victim?

Or are we loyal to Love?

Are we loyal to Truth?

Are we loyal to Forgiveness?

Are we loyal to Gratitude?

Are we loyal to Healing?

Are we loyal to Oneness?

Are we loyal to Service?

These are not merely ideas. They are choices we make every day.

This is where Loyalty connects naturally with the other virtues. Humility helps us release the ego. Loyalty helps us remain faithful to what is higher than the ego. Courage will be required when that loyalty is tested. Positive Living will determine the energy we bring into our commitments. Truthfulness will keep loyalty from becoming dishonesty. Self-Control will keep us from breaking commitments whenever our emotions change. Purity will ask us to examine the motives behind our promises.

The Seven Power Virtues are not separate boxes.

They are seven expressions of the same spiritual life.

Loyalty also requires courage because there will be times when standing by what is right will cost us something. It may cost us popularity. It may cost us a friendship. It may cost us money. It may cost us our position in an organization. It may even cost us the approval of people we love.

That is why loyalty without courage is fragile.

It is easy to be loyal when everyone agrees with us.

The real test comes when standing for truth becomes uncomfortable.

There are people who have kept their promises for decades, not because every day was easy, but because they understood that commitment is deeper than a passing emotion. They understood that love is not merely what we feel. Love is also what we do.

That is why loyalty is one of the highest forms of love.

Love says, "I see you."

Loyalty says, "I will not casually abandon you."

Love says, "You matter."

Loyalty says, "Your importance does not disappear when life becomes difficult."

Love says, "I want what is good for you."

Loyalty says, "I will remain faithful to that good, even when it requires courage."

But the highest loyalty of all is loyalty to the Divine within ourselves and within all beings.

There may come a time when every human identity falls away. Our titles disappear. Our possessions disappear. Our accomplishments become memories. Even our relationships change. What remains is the deeper truth of who we are.

If we have been loyal to that truth throughout our lives, then we have not lived in vain.

To be loyal to the Divine is to be loyal to Love when hatred would be easier. To be loyal to Truth when lying would be more convenient. To be loyal to Forgiveness when resentment feels justified. To be loyal to Gratitude when life disappoints us. To be loyal to Healing when we would rather remain a victim. To be loyal to Oneness when the ego wants separation. And to be loyal to Service when the ego wants recognition.

This is a different kind of loyalty.

It is not loyalty to a person, institution or identity.

It is loyalty to the deepest truth of our own being.

And perhaps this is where the virtue of Loyalty finally brings us: not toward greater attachment, but toward greater freedom.

When we become loyal to the Divine, we become less dependent upon the approval of the world. We no longer have to follow every changing opinion. We do not have to betray our conscience to belong. We can stand quietly in what we know to be true while still respecting those who see life differently.

We become trustworthy because we are no longer divided within ourselves.

Our word begins to mean something.

Our commitments begin to carry weight.

Our relationships become deeper.

Our friendships become more meaningful.

Our families know they can count on us.

And our spiritual life becomes something we actually live rather than something we merely talk about.

Loyalty is ultimately a practice of remembering.

Remembering our promises.

Remembering those who have loved us.

Remembering our responsibilities.

Remembering our values.

And, above all, remembering who we really are.

We are not separate beings wandering through a disconnected universe.

We are expressions of One Life.

When we understand this, loyalty becomes more than duty. It becomes Love in action.

We remain faithful not because we are afraid to leave, but because we have chosen what is worthy of our devotion.

We keep our word because our word carries the energy of our character.

We honor our commitments because commitment shapes the soul.

And we remain loyal to the Divine because, beneath all our differences, the Divine is what we are.

Humility teaches us to bow.

Loyalty teaches us to stand.

And the spiritual life requires both.
