Purity

Returning to a Clear Heart
By Rev. Bill McDonald · 7 min read

About This Reflection

Rev. Bill presents purity as sincerity, not perfection or moral judgment. It is the clearing away of resentment, false motive and mental noise — the integration of everything the six virtues before it have been preparing.

Purity is often misunderstood. We may think of it as being innocent, perfect, or somehow untouched by the difficulties of life. But true purity has little to do with outward appearances. It is a condition of the heart and consciousness.

To live with purity is to live with a clean heart, a clear mind, and honest intentions. It is to become increasingly aware of what we allow to enter our consciousness and what we carry within it. Purity is not about judging ourselves or others. It is about becoming free of the thoughts, desires, resentments, fears, and attachments that cloud our connection with the Divine.

The ancient Eastern teachings have always pointed us inward. Beneath the personality, beneath the ego, beneath the endless activity of the mind, there is a deeper Self. This Self is not something we manufacture. It is something we discover. It is the quiet presence that has been with us all along.

Purity is the clearing away of what prevents us from recognizing that presence.

A pure heart does not mean a heart that has never been hurt, angry, afraid, or confused. We are human beings, and life leaves its impressions upon us. Purity means that we do not allow those impressions to become our identity. We learn to observe them, understand them, forgive them, and release them.

The mind can become crowded with old stories. We remember who hurt us, what someone said, what we lost, what should have happened, what we believe we deserve. We carry these things forward and then wonder why we cannot experience peace. Purity begins when we become willing to empty some of this unnecessary baggage.

The spiritual path is not always about adding more knowledge. Sometimes it is about removing what is false.

We can read thousands of books, meditate for years, visit temples and churches, listen to teachers, study scriptures, and speak endlessly about enlightenment. Yet if our hearts remain filled with hatred, jealousy, dishonesty, greed, judgment, or selfish desire, we have missed the essence of the journey.

Purity asks us to look honestly at ourselves.

What are our motives? Why do we do what we do? What are we seeking from other people? Are we acting from love or from fear? Are we serving the Divine, or are we simply trying to strengthen the ego?

These are not questions meant to condemn us. They are invitations to become conscious.

The Eastern traditions speak of the purification of consciousness because consciousness itself is sacred. The more we quiet the noise of the ego, the more clearly, we begin to see. We discover that beneath our changing thoughts and emotions is something still, something spacious, something untouched.

That is where the journey becomes deeply spiritual.

Purity is not perfection. It is sincerity.

It is being willing to live truthfully. It is having the courage to examine our thoughts before they become words, and our words before they become actions. It is choosing what we feed our minds just as carefully as we choose what we feed our bodies.

Every thought leaves an impression.

Every word carries energy.

Every action strengthens a pattern.

If we continually feed ourselves negativity, resentment, gossip, fear, and judgment, we should not be surprised when our consciousness becomes heavy. But when we cultivate love, compassion, forgiveness, gratitude, kindness, truth, and self-awareness, something within us begins to change.

This is why purity belongs at the end of these Seven Power Virtues.

Humility teaches us that the ego does not know everything. Loyalty teaches us faithfulness and devotion. Courage teaches us to walk through fear. Positive Living teaches us to choose the light rather than continually dwelling in darkness. Self-Control teaches us mastery over our impulses. And Purity asks us to bring the heart and mind into alignment with the Divine.

Purity is the integration of all the others.

A humble heart becomes less interested in proving itself. A loyal heart learns devotion. A courageous heart faces what it has avoided. A positive heart looks for possibility rather than constantly feeding negativity. A disciplined heart learns not to be ruled by every desire.

And gradually, the inner waters become clearer.

When the water is muddy, we cannot see the bottom. When the water becomes still and clear, we can see deeply into it.

The same is true of consciousness.

We do not have to create the Divine within us. We have to become quiet enough to recognize what has always been there.

This is the deeper meaning of purity: returning to our original nature.

The mystics of many traditions have pointed toward the same truth—that the Divine is not somewhere far away waiting for us to become worthy. The Divine is already present. The journey is one of awakening, remembering, and removing the layers that have covered our awareness of it.

Purity therefore becomes a daily practice.

Watch your thoughts. Watch your words. Watch your desires. Watch how you treat people when you have nothing to gain from them. Watch what happens inside you when someone disagrees with you. Watch your reaction when life does not give you what you want.

These moments reveal us.

And what they reveal is not meant to shame us. It is simply where the work is.

The pure heart is not the heart that never encounters darkness. It is the heart that refuses to become darkness.

We can experience anger without becoming anger. We can experience fear without becoming fear. We can experience desire without allowing desire to rule us. We can experience pain without turning pain into hatred.

This is freedom.

Purity is also the purification of desire. There is nothing wrong with having desires, dreams, relationships, possessions, or ambitions. The question is whether they possess us. When we believe that something outside ourselves must change before we can be whole, we give away our freedom.

The spiritual seeker gradually discovers another way.

We begin to want what is real rather than merely what is attractive to the ego. We begin to desire truth more than approval, peace more than victory, love more than control, and awakening more than recognition.

Our desires become simpler because our consciousness becomes clearer.

Eventually, purity becomes less about controlling ourselves and more about knowing ourselves.

We begin to recognize the difference between the voice of the ego and the quiet knowing of the deeper Self. The ego is often loud, demanding and afraid of losing its identity. The inner Self does not need to shout. It simply knows.

This is why meditation, prayer, contemplation, silence, nature, service, and honest self-examination can be so powerful. They give us moments when we can step outside the constant movement of the personality and remember that we are more than our thoughts.

Purity is the return to that remembrance.

It is a clean heart.

It is an honest intention.

It is a quiet mind.

It is a consciousness becoming transparent to the Divine.

And perhaps, in the end, purity is simply becoming who we truly are beneath everything we have learned to be.

The journey of the Seven Power Virtues is therefore not a journey toward becoming a perfect human being. It is a journey toward becoming a more conscious human being.

As consciousness becomes clearer, love becomes more natural. Forgiveness becomes easier. Gratitude becomes deeper. Service becomes less about recognition. Humility becomes natural because we begin to see that the same Divine presence lives within everyone.

Purity leads us back to Oneness.

There is no separation between the spiritual life and ordinary life. How we think, how we speak, how we love, how we forgive, how we treat another human being, and how we respond when life challenges us are all part of the spiritual path.

The sacred is not waiting somewhere else.

It is here.

It is within you.

Purity is learning to see it.

And when the heart becomes clear enough to see the Divine in ourselves and in one another, we discover that the greatest purification was never about becoming something we were not.

It was about removing everything that kept us from remembering who we have always been.
