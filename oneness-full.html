The Six Pillars · Full Teaching

Oneness — There Is Only One

If LOVE is the first doorway, FORGIVENESS releases us from what we have carried, GRATITUDE teaches us to receive life, and HEALING brings us back toward wholeness, then ONENESS is the realization toward which the entire journey has been moving.

It is the great remembering.

Beneath all appearances, names, bodies, personalities, religions, cultures, and identities, there is only ONE. One Source. One Life. One Consciousness. One Divine Presence. One Love.

We give that One many names, God, Brahman, the Divine, the Source, Universal Consciousness, the Great Spirit. The names change. The One does not.

The human mind naturally divides the world. This is me. That is you. This is sacred. That is ordinary. But the mystic begins to question these divisions. If the Divine is truly infinite, where could the Divine end?

The ancient teachings of the Upanishads explored this mystery through the relationship between Atman, the deepest Self, and Brahman, the ultimate Reality. What if there has never been any real separation?

Imagine the ocean. A wave rises from its surface. It has a shape, a beginning, a moment when it stands high above the water. Then it returns. Is the wave something separate from the ocean? Its form is temporary, but its substance is the ocean. The wave does not need to become the ocean. It already is the ocean, appearing as a wave.

Perhaps this is something like who we are. We appear as individual human beings, with names, bodies, personalities, memories, dreams, and different journeys through life. Yet beneath the temporary form is something deeper. The same Source. The same Life. The same Divine mystery.

We are waves of the One Ocean. And so is everyone else.

This is why LOVE begins with self-love. We are being invited to discover what is sacred within us, and then to recognize that same sacred Presence in another. The friend. The stranger. The child. The elder. Even the person who has hurt us.

If there is truly only One, then perhaps there is no ultimate other. There are countless expressions, but one Source. Countless faces, but one Life. Many forms. One Essence.

The differences between us are real in ordinary human life. ONENESS does not ask us to pretend otherwise. It asks us to look deeper. Many waves. One Ocean. Many lamps. One Light.

Much of human suffering begins when separation becomes the deepest truth we know. We create us and them, my side and your side, my people and everyone else. And once we convince ourselves that another person is completely separate from us, it becomes easier to judge, hate, harm, or simply stop caring about their suffering.

ONENESS interrupts that illusion. It asks us to look again. Not to deny our differences, but to look beneath them. What Life is breathing through both of us? What mystery gave birth to us both?

Many people spend their lives searching for God, traveling to sacred places, studying scriptures, following teachers. There is great beauty in all of these paths. But eventually, the mystic turns inward. Because what if the One we have been searching for has never been absent? What if every breath is already happening within the Divine?

Perhaps the greatest spiritual journey is not a journey toward God. It is the realization that we have never been outside God.

This does not mean that every human action is good, or that ONENESS asks us to abandon moral discernment. Cruelty is still cruelty. Harm is still harm. But ONENESS gives us an even deeper reason to live with compassion. If there is one Life, then what I do matters. How I treat another matters. How I treat the Earth matters.

If I believe you are completely separate from me, then loving you may seem like a choice I make. But when I recognize that we arise from the same Divine Source, Love becomes something closer to recognition. We may still establish boundaries. We may still confront injustice. But we no longer need hatred to become our identity.

ONENESS also deepens FORGIVENESS. When someone hurts us, the ego immediately emphasizes separation: you did this to me. Forgiveness does not say that the harm was acceptable. It says: I will not allow what happened to convince me that hatred is my true nature. The boundary may remain. But inwardly, we can release the poison. We return to Love. We return to ourselves. We return to the One.

GRATITUDE also becomes deeper through ONENESS. We did not create the air we breathe, or manufacture the sun, or invent the Earth beneath our feet. When we begin seeing ourselves as expressions of the One, gratitude becomes a natural response.

HEALING also takes on a deeper meaning: I am more than the condition I am experiencing. The Divine Presence is deeper than the circumstance.

And eventually, ONENESS naturally leads us toward SERVICE. This is where mystical understanding becomes practical. If there is truly only One, then helping another is no longer simply an act of generosity. It becomes an expression of our deepest nature. Not: I am better than you, so I will help you. But: we belong to one another.

Eventually, ONENESS becomes difficult to explain because words themselves create divisions. The mystic eventually moves beyond words, into silence, into meditation, into the space between thoughts. And there, perhaps, something becomes clear. Before our names. Before our identities. There was simply Being. Life. Consciousness. The One.

Perhaps human life is, in part, a great forgetting. We forget our sacred nature. We become fascinated with our temporary identities. We compare. We judge. We divide. We separate. And then something happens: a sunrise, a birth, a death, a great love, a mountain, a song, a moment of profound silence. And suddenly, if only for a moment, the walls disappear. We feel connected. We feel whole. We remember that we belong.

This is why the Six Pillars are not truly six separate teachings. They are six movements of the same awakening.

LOVE teaches us to recognize the Divine within ourselves and others. FORGIVENESS helps us release what keeps us imprisoned in the past. GRATITUDE teaches us to receive the mystery of life with an open heart. HEALING brings the divided parts of ourselves back toward wholeness. ONENESS reveals that beneath all our differences, we arise from the same Divine Source. And SERVICE asks us to live that realization in the world.

At the deepest level, there may be nowhere to go and nothing we need to become. There is no distant God waiting at the end of a spiritual road. There is only the gradual removal of what has obscured what was always present: fear, judgment, hatred, shame, attachment, the illusion of separation.

And what remains? LOVE.

God is Love. And if God is everywhere, then Love is not confined to beautiful places, pleasant experiences, or the people we find easiest to love. The Divine is present throughout existence. The One is not divided.

Perhaps someday we stop saying: I want to find God. And begin asking: Where could God possibly not be?

Different in appearance. One in essence.

And when this realization begins moving from the mind into the heart, everything changes. We love differently. We forgive differently. We receive life differently. We heal differently. We serve differently. We see differently. Because we are no longer looking at life as a collection of completely separate things. We begin to see ONE LIFE appearing in countless forms.

There is only One. Call it God. Call it Brahman. Call it the Divine. Call it Consciousness. Call it Love. The name matters far less than the realization.

There is no place where the Divine is absent. There is no life outside the One. There is no creation separate from its Source.

We love because there is One. We forgive because there is One. We give thanks because there is One. We heal because there is One. And we serve because there is One.

And finally, we understand that the spiritual journey was never really about finding our way to the Divine. It was about awakening to the truth that we have never been anywhere else.

— Rev. Bill McDonald
