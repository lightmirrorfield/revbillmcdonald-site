Forgiveness — The Freedom to Set the Heart Free

If LOVE is the first doorway into the Divine, then FORGIVENESS is one of the ways we learn to walk through life without carrying the weight of yesterday.

Every human life contains experiences that leave their mark. Words are spoken that cannot be taken back. Promises are broken. Trust is betrayed. Relationships wound us. Sometimes the deepest pain does not come from strangers, but from those we loved, trusted, and believed would never hurt us.

We cannot always choose what happens to us. But eventually, we must decide what we are going to continue carrying.

This is where forgiveness begins.

Forgiveness is not primarily a gift we give to another person. It is a gift we give ourselves. It is the decision to stop allowing something that happened in the past to continue occupying the sacred space of the present.

When we hold tightly to anger, resentment, hatred, or the desire for revenge, we may believe we are holding someone else accountable. But often, we are the ones carrying the burden. The person who hurt us may have moved on, while we continue replaying the conversation, revisiting the betrayal, and imagining what we should have said or done differently.

Each time we repeat the old story, we give the past more of our present life.

Forgiveness interrupts that pattern. It says: I will remember what happened, but I will no longer allow what happened to own me.

That is freedom.

Forgiveness does not mean pretending that the past did not matter. It means the past no longer has the authority to determine the condition of our heart. When we release the grip of old anger, we reclaim energy that has been trapped in yesterday. We create room for peace, healing, joy, and the possibility of something new.

Forgiveness also does not mean forgetting.

There are things we should remember, not so that we remain angry, but so that we become wiser. Every experience has something to teach us. Sometimes we learn where our boundaries need to be. Sometimes we learn to trust our intuition. Sometimes we discover that we ignored what we already knew because we desperately wanted a relationship to work.

We can remember without reliving. We can understand without condemning. We can become wiser without becoming harder.

Forgiveness allows us to keep the wisdom without continuing to carry the wound.

Nor does forgiveness require reconciliation. There are people we can forgive whom we should not invite back into our lives. There are relationships that ended for good reasons. We can forgive someone and still maintain a boundary. We can love someone and choose distance. We can wish them well without giving them continued access to our lives.

Compassion does not require access. Forgiveness is an inward spiritual act. Separation may still be the wise outward action. We can release hatred without surrendering discernment.

We do not even have to announce our forgiveness. Sometimes the deepest forgiveness happens quietly, with no audience and no ceremony. It may come while sitting alone in meditation, walking in nature, or during a quiet moment when we suddenly realize that we no longer feel the need to rehearse the old story.

One day we may discover something remarkable: we remember what happened, but it no longer hurts in the same way. The past has become a teacher rather than a prison.

As with LOVE, forgiveness must also begin with ourselves. Many of us carry old accusations against ourselves for years. I should have known better. Why did I do that? Why wasn't I stronger? Why didn't I leave sooner?

We can become far harsher toward ourselves than we would ever be toward another human being.

But if LOVE teaches us to meet ourselves with compassion, FORGIVENESS asks us to extend that compassion toward our own history. We were learning. We were growing. We made choices with the consciousness we possessed at the time. That is part of being human.

The purpose of spiritual awakening is not to create a perfect human being. It is to become a more conscious one.

Forgiveness gives the soul room to breathe. We can spend years filling ourselves with old anger, guilt, shame, disappointments, and judgments about who we once were. Then we wonder why there seems to be no room for peace or something new.

Forgiveness is the willingness to put some of that burden down, not because our experiences never mattered, but because we no longer need to live inside them.

We stop demanding that the past become different. It cannot. We stop waiting for another person to become who we hoped they would be. They may never change. We stop believing that our peace depends upon receiving an apology. That apology may never come.

And slowly, we discover a deeper truth: my freedom cannot depend upon another person's behavior.

They may never understand what they did. They may never acknowledge the pain they caused. They may never apologize. And still, we can become free.

This is where forgiveness becomes a profound act of spiritual independence. We take our life back.

I believe we are called to LOVE everyone because the Divine lives within everyone. But loving someone does not mean approving of their behavior. Love and approval are not the same thing. Forgiveness and trust are not the same thing. Compassion and permission are not the same thing.

We can recognize the Divine within another human being and still say, I cannot allow your behavior into my life. There is no contradiction in that. There is wisdom.

There may come a time when forgiveness is no longer something we are trying to accomplish. It simply begins to happen. The memory remains. The wisdom remains. Perhaps even the boundary remains. But the emotional charge slowly loses its power.

We release them, not because they earned our forgiveness, but because we finally understand that our soul deserves freedom.

Forgiveness is not weakness. It can require tremendous courage. It takes courage to release anger when anger has become familiar. And perhaps the greatest courage of all is learning to forgive ourselves.

I choose peace over bitterness. I choose wisdom over resentment. I choose freedom over attachment to the past.

And when I am not yet ready to forgive, I can begin by forgiving myself for not being ready. Healing has its own rhythm. Forgiveness is not a race, and it cannot always be forced. It is a sacred unfolding.

Eventually, we may discover that the person we thought we were freeing was not the only one who needed freedom. We did too.

This is the quiet power of FORGIVENESS. Not forgetting. Not excusing. Not abandoning wisdom. Not reopening every door. But releasing what no longer belongs in the sacred space of our inner life.

We carry forward the wisdom, but we put down the burden. We learn. We release. We love. And we continue walking toward the Light.

— Rev. Bill McDonald
