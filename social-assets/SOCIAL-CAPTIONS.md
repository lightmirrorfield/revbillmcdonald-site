## Spiritual Performance vs. Spiritual Transformation
Link: https://revbillmcdonald.com/reflections/spiritual-performance-vs-transformation/

### One-line teaser (14 words)
Rev. Bill McDonald on the difference between appearing spiritual and being transformed by spirituality.

### Instagram caption (129 words)

In this reflection, Rev. Bill McDonald writes about a danger on the spiritual path that is rarely discussed: becoming attached to the image of being spiritual. Meditation, prayer, sacred teachings, ceremony and retreat can all open a door. But opening the door is not the same thing as walking through it.

He invites us to ask more honest questions. Am I becoming more loving? More honest? More forgiving? Humbler? Or have I simply become very good at looking spiritual?

“The goal is not to become spiritually special. The goal is to become real.”

### Facebook / LinkedIn caption (193 words)
What if the greatest spiritual question is not what we have experienced, but what those experiences have changed within us?

Rev. Bill McDonald’s reflection, Spiritual Performance vs. Spiritual Transformation, begins with a warning that is easy to overlook: the ego is clever, and it can turn almost anything, even spirituality, into another reason to feel important, special or separate. Meditation, prayer, study and ceremony are not wrong. They can open a door. But Rev. Bill asks what is actually changing inside us once we walk through it.

Are we becoming more loving, more honest, more forgiving, humbler? Does our practice change how we treat people in the kitchen, in the marriage, at work, and with those who disagree with us? When spirituality comes home, he writes, life itself becomes the ashram.

He also reminds us that spiritual experiences and powers are not the same thing as wisdom, and that the real path may be simpler than the ego wants it to be: love more, forgive more, tell the truth, serve others, and keep looking inward.

“The goal is not to become spiritually special. The goal is to become real.”

### Facebook / LinkedIn caption (182 words)
There is a subtle difference between feeling called to serve and needing to believe we were chosen because we are spiritually special.

Rev. Bill McDonald’s reflection, The Chosen One Trap, does not question spiritual calling itself. Human beings have always felt called to serve something larger than themselves, and a genuine calling can open the heart. The danger begins when that feeling turns into an identity, such as “I have been selected” or “I am here to save people,” and places us above the people we believe we have come to help.

Rev. Bill offers questions rather than accusations. How do I know? Could I be mistaking my own desires for Divine instructions? If I never receive recognition, do I still want to serve? If someone else is celebrated, can I genuinely celebrate them? He contrasts the savior, who needs someone to save, with the servant, who simply serves and encourages another person’s own strength and freedom.

“Never confuse being useful with being superior.”

Perhaps the question changes from “Why was I chosen?” to “How can I serve?”

Rev. Bill McDonald’s reflection, Being a Spiritual Householder, begins with a common misunderstanding: that the serious seeker must leave ordinary life behind for an ashram, a monastery or a cave. He recalls the ancient Indian view of the householder, where the home itself could become a place of dharma, discipline, love and service, and the example of Lahiri Mahasaya, a married man, a father and an employee who practiced and taught the highest principles of yoga.

Rev. Bill asks whether marriage can become a school of patience, children teachers of unconditional love, and work an opportunity for service. And he reminds us that meditation does not end when the eyes open.

“Perhaps the greatest gift we can give the next generation is not another lesson about spirituality. Perhaps it is to become spiritual ourselves.”

There are mountains throughout the world that human beings have considered sacred, and Rev. Bill McDonald is clear that he is not trying to create an official list of them.
