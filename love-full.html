Love — The Foundation of the Spiritual Life

Love is not simply one of the qualities we hope to develop on the spiritual path. Love is the path itself.

Before forgiveness can become real, before gratitude becomes a way of life, before healing can take place, before we recognize our Oneness, and before service becomes a natural expression of the heart, we must begin with the most intimate relationship we will ever have — the relationship with ourselves.

This is where the journey begins.

One of the great misunderstandings of spiritual life is that we are often taught to love everyone else while quietly carrying judgment, disappointment, shame, or rejection toward ourselves. We may speak about loving God, humanity, nature, and the world, while somewhere inside there is still a voice saying, I am not enough. I am not worthy.

But how can we fully recognize the Divine in another if we continually deny the sacred presence within ourselves?

Self-love is not narcissism, nor is it the glorification of the ego. It is something much deeper. It is learning to meet ourselves with compassion. It is forgiving ourselves for what we did not know, recognizing our wounds without becoming defined by them, and finally ending the habit of treating ourselves as our own enemy.

As that compassion grows, something begins to change. The distance between me and you becomes less solid.

The great spiritual traditions of the East have pointed toward this understanding for thousands of years. Beneath our names, histories, personalities, successes, and failures, there is something deeper. The ancient teachings of Atman and Brahman speak of a Divine reality that transcends the illusion of separation.

I call that reality the Divine Presence.

And if that Presence lives within me, where does it end? Does it stop at the edge of my skin? Does it disappear when I meet someone who disagrees with me — or someone I find difficult to love?

Or is that same sacred Presence there as well?

This is where Love becomes more than an emotion. It becomes a spiritual realization.

As we learn to love ourselves with genuine compassion, we begin to see beyond the stories we have created about who we are. The need to constantly prove ourselves grows quieter. Comparison loses some of its power. The heart becomes less guarded.

And because we begin seeing ourselves differently, we begin seeing others differently too.

The stranger becomes another human being. The difficult person becomes someone carrying wounds and experiences we may never understand. Even those we have called enemies present us with a profound spiritual question: Can I still recognize the Divine here?

This does not mean accepting cruelty or allowing harmful behavior. Love does not ask us to abandon discernment or healthy boundaries. We can walk away when necessary. We can oppose injustice. We can protect ourselves. But we do not have to allow hatred and vengeance to take possession of our hearts.

If God is truly the Source of all existence, then there is nowhere for God to be absent.

The Divine is not confined to a church, a temple, a book, or a particular belief. It is present in life itself — in the mountains and oceans, in children and elders, in those we admire, and in those we struggle to understand.

The spiritual journey is not about creating God within ourselves. It is about becoming still enough to recognize what has always been there.

When this realization enters the heart, Love naturally expands into Oneness.

Oneness does not mean sameness. We do not have to think alike, believe alike, worship alike, or live alike. Our differences remain. But beneath those differences is something deeper: one Life, one Source, one Divine mystery expressing itself through countless forms.

This is why LOVE is the first of my Six Pillars.

Love opens the way to FORGIVENESS, because a heart that recognizes our shared humanity becomes less interested in keeping score.

Love opens us to GRATITUDE, because we begin to see life itself as a sacred gift.

Love supports HEALING, because we stop fighting ourselves and begin bringing compassion to the wounded places within us.

Love reveals ONENESS, because we begin to recognize that the Divine within ourselves is not separate from the Divine within another.

And Love naturally becomes SERVICE, because when we understand that there is only one sacred Whole, serving another is no longer simply helping someone outside of ourselves. It becomes an offering to the Divine present in all things.

The practice is simple, though living it can take a lifetime.

Begin with yourself. Instead of continually asking, What is wrong with me?, ask, Can I meet myself with compassion?

Can I forgive the younger person I once was? Can I stop demanding perfection from someone who is still learning? Can I recognize that beneath all my successes and failures, there is something sacred within me?

Then carry that awareness into the world. Look beyond another person's personality, their words, their wounds — and ask whether you can still recognize the Divine there.

— Rev. Bill McDonald
