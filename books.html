Eight Books

Books From the Journey

Memoir, essays, prayers, and five decades of poetry — written across sixty years and published in English, German, and Spanish.

2025

Cosmic Chronicles

A collection of spiritual essays drawing on his life as a Vietnam veteran, healer, poet, and near-death experiencer. He writes on overcoming fear, the power of forgiveness, reincarnation, and the energy of love — bridging the metaphysical and the practical in plain, accessible language.

2024

Spiritual Prayers & Blessings

Original prayers and blessings that cross the boundaries of any single religion, addressed to the one Creator and written for anyone regardless of faith background. Intended as a companion for life’s significant moments — a funeral, a wedding, a home blessing, a season of healing — for those who want the words and cannot find them.

2024

Sacred Verses

Poetry and prose as a record of a spiritual journey. He describes these pieces as passageways into his own interior — written from the space between thoughts and words, moving through joy, peace, pain, and suffering toward a single theme of oneness.

Find it on Amazon →
2024

I Remember Tomorrow

An account of the last decade: cancer, heart attacks, and the upheaval of the pandemic, held alongside the practice of self-love and self-forgiveness as the ground of any real healing. The travel runs from India to Germany, England to Switzerland and the Czech Republic, and the personal stories return again and again to the same two subjects.

Find it on Amazon →
2018

Alchemy of a Warrior’s Heart

The follow-up to Warrior. An old Vietnam veteran works out why he is here, across four journeys to India and months at a time in isolation at a jungle ashram with his newly found Satguru. The memoir covers the decade after he retired from government work.

Find it on Amazon →
2015

Warrior: A Spiritual Odyssey

The autobiography. It begins in a difficult childhood, moves through an astonishing tour of duty in Vietnam, and opens out into a life of unusual breadth. Selected stories rather than a straight chronology — and it ends, as his own search did, standing at Machu Picchu.
