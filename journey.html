The Full Story

The Journey

Eight decades. A childhood he had to survive, a war he was not supposed to come home from, and a message he has spent the rest of his life carrying.

Rev. Bill McDonald was born in San Francisco in 1946 and raised across California, Oregon, and Hawaii. He describes the early years as a long stretch of instability — a childhood that ended, as he tells it, somewhere over the jungle canopy of Vietnam.

What follows is his life in four movements. He would be the first to say the chapters overlap, and that the warrior never entirely left.

Chapter One · Vietnam, Late 1960s

The Warrior

He served as a helicopter crew chief and door gunner with the “Tomahawks” of the 128th Assault Helicopter Company. It was a role with one of the shortest life expectancies of the war — sitting in an open doorway, exposed, while the aircraft went into places nothing else would go.

He came home decorated and, by his own account, unfinished. The medals recognized what he had done. They had nothing to say about what he had seen, or about the strange moments during that tour that he could not explain and would spend the next fifty years trying to understand.

Decorations

  • Distinguished Flying Cross
  • Bronze Star
  • Purple Heart
  • 14 Air Medals
  • Vietnamese Cross of Gallantry
The war gave him the question. It took the rest of his life to find the answer.
Chapter Two · The Years After

The Seeker

He came back to an ordinary American life — the kind that looks, from the outside, like recovery. He married Carol, his high school sweetheart, in 1970. They settled in Elk Grove, California, in 1979, and have been there ever since. He earned an associate degree in labor management from San Jose City College and a bachelor’s degree in public service administration from the University of San Francisco. He spent more than thirty years working for the federal government and retired in 2001.

Underneath the ordinary life, the search continued. He read, he questioned, he sought out teachers. He describes three near-death and near-death-like experiences across his life, and countless encounters he can only call supernatural. He did not go looking for them. They kept arriving.

Chapter Three · India and Beyond

The Mystic

Four times he traveled to India. He spent months at a stretch in isolation at a jungle ashram with the teacher he came to call his Satguru. He has written about experiences during those years that he associates with Mahavatar Babaji — accounts he offers as his own personal testimony rather than as doctrine, and which readers are free to take as they find them.

He has spoken and taught in Germany, England, Wales, Bolivia, Switzerland, the Czech Republic, and across the United States. His books have been published in German and Spanish. What he says he learned, after all of it, is disarmingly plain: the only thing that is finally real is love.

There is but ONE GOD — and our only mission is to LOVE & SERVE ALL.
Chapter Four · Today

The Messenger

Author, award-winning poet, minister, speaker, film adviser, and veterans advocate. Since retiring he has worked with five different nonprofit organizations serving veterans, and serves as an unpaid lobbyist on veterans’ issues in California. He has worked with veterans living with PTSD, volunteered as a chaplain in Folsom Prison, and spent time answering calls for the national suicide hotline.

He has appeared on more than 800 radio and television programs, been involved with a dozen films and documentaries — among them In the Shadow of the Blade on the History Channel and The Art of Healing on PBS — and had his story told in over a dozen books by other authors and in more than a hundred magazine and newspaper articles, in publications as far apart as Parade and the Self-Realization Fellowship Magazine. His YouTube channel drew more than half a million views in its first eighteen months.

The last decade brought cancer, heart attacks, and the long dislocation of the pandemic. He writes about all of it. He treats a homeless man and a head of state exactly the same way, and says the reason is simple: he cannot see a difference.

The message is more important than the messenger.

That sentence is the closest thing he has to a creed, and the reason this site exists at all. He is not asking anyone to follow him. He is asking them to take the message and carry it into the decades after he is gone.
