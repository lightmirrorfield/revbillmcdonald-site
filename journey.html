His Life Story

The Journey

From childhood visions to Vietnam, from near-death to the Himalayas, and the message that came after

Rev. Bill McDonald was born March 16, 1946, in San Francisco. His childhood was marked by poverty, neglect, and illness. At eight years old he had the first of three near-death experiences, and afterward began having visions — some of which, he says, showed events that would not happen for years, including glimpses of the Kennedy assassination and the war he would later fight in.

Vietnam, 1966–1967

The Warrior

In 1966 and 1967 he served in Vietnam as a crew chief and door gunner on a Huey helicopter, flying combat operations out of Phu Loi. His aircraft was shot down more than once. He was wounded. He came home with the Distinguished Flying Cross, the Bronze Star, a Purple Heart, fourteen Air Medals, and the Vietnamese Gallantry Cross.

The war taught him something entirely different from what he expected. Combat, death, survival, fear, courage, and loss became the beginning of a spiritual transformation rather than the end of one.

The Years After

The Seeker

He came home and went to work. Beginning in 1970 he volunteered with Vietnam veterans carrying what would later be named PTSD, developing programs and approaches long before the condition had a name. That work eventually contributed to a PTSD program adopted by the Veterans Administration in 2004.

He helped produce the PBS program “The Art of Healing,” and served as crew member and chaplain for the documentary “In the Shadow of the Blade,” which followed a restored Vietnam-era Huey and the veterans connected to it.

India · The Himalayas

The Mystic

India shaped him. Across many journeys he sought out holy men, rishis, sages, gurus, healers, and sacred places. His relationship with the Himalayan master Babaji has been central.

In 2004, fifty years to the day after his childhood near-death experience, he returned from Babaji’s Cave in the Himalayas, suffered a massive heart attack, and fell roughly thirty feet from a cliff onto a boulder. He describes watching his own body from outside it, and a large cobra crossing his legs. Instead of fear he felt overwhelming love — and believes the encounter returned him to his body.

In 2011, after another heart attack in India, he underwent open-heart surgery in California. During the eight-hour operation he describes being spiritually present in India while his body remained on the table. He was shown a choice: peace and release, or return to a life with more suffering and more chance to serve.

He chose to return. He says he was shown thousands of faces of people he would one day meet — and has since recognized some of them at his own retreats.

Decades of Service

The Messenger

He founded The Spiritual Warrior Ministries. For most of the last thirty years he has taught without charge, or for donation only. He is direct about why: this is not a business. It is his mission of love.

In January 2023 he began a three-year teaching journey in Santa Cruz, California. It carried him across the United States and to England, Wales, Germany, Switzerland, the Czech Republic, and India, and ended as he turned eighty in March 2026.

He has been interviewed on nearly two thousand programs over twenty-four years, appeared in a dozen films and documentaries, keynoted the IANDS conference in 2019, and been written about in roughly twenty-five books by other authors. Videos of his talks have passed three million views.

He returns, always, to the same correction: the numbers are not the important part. The people are.

What Comes Next

Having finished the three-year journey at eighty, he is turning toward mentoring — training younger teachers who can carry the work forward. His hope is not to create followers. It is to create teachers.
